Question · answered from Nathan Lambert's material · reference example

Why does RLVR often omit the KL penalty?

The KL penalty exists to stop the optimizer exploiting a learned reward model. In RLVR the reward is a check against a verifiable answer, so there is less to exploit, and removing the penalty lets long runs explore further. Lambert limits this to basic math and code: as runs lengthen and tool use grows, regularization is coming back. 1 4 6

What Lambert says

What the penalty is for. In RLHF the reward model is a learned proxy, and without regularization the policy exploits its imperfections. 2 In practice that meant the optimizer moving stylistic features until the model stopped making sense. 3

Why RLVR needs it less. A reader proposed that verifiable rewards avoid the proxy problem, and Lambert's answer was "Overall yes", adding that Tülu 3 models could learn math without out-of-distribution degradation. 1 His slide states the general trend: more reliable reward signals mean less need for KL regularization. 2

What removing it buys. The book gives a second reason beside the reward: RL runs got longer, and dropping the constraint to the base model lets the model explore further during training. 4 Open-Reasoner-Zero, Magistral, Skywork OR-1, DAPO and GLM-5 all dropped it. 5

Where the rule stops. He applies "you do not need a KL penalty" to basic math and code RLVR. With tool use, over-optimization takes more forms and regularization is returning. 6 Very long runs may also bring the penalty back, to keep the model grounded near where it started. 7

Where his sources differ

unresolved DeepSeek R1. Three of Lambert's own sources describe it differently. His material does not settle this. The R1 paper would: the Q&A 3 slide cites it as guo2025deepseek.

unresolved What is coming back. Lecture 5 (June 2026) says long-horizon agentic training is reviving a KL or reference term. 9 Lecture 10 (July 2026) says regularization is returning but aimed at drift from the sampling distribution, "not a KL penalty to a reference model". 8 These may describe different training setups, or a change in his view; the slides alone do not say which.
