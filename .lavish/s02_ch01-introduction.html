Chapter 1: Introduction

RLHF teaches a language model what people prefer by using comparisons rather than a written rule. This chapter sets up the book: what RLHF changes in a model, the three-step recipe from OpenAI's InstructGPT, and why RLHF is now one tool inside a larger process called post-training. 1 2 3

After this chapter you should be able to

  • Say what each of the three recipe steps produces.
  • Tell instruction tuning, preference tuning and RLVR apart.
  • Answer "isn't RLHF just style?"
  • Explain the elicitation theory, and where it stops working.

RLHF in three steps

In Lecture 1: 23:55

RLHF brings human information into AI systems. It started as a way to solve problems that are hard to specify, because a person's preferences are often hard to express as a rule. 1

The basic pipeline has three steps: train a model that can follow questions, collect human preferences to train a reward model, then optimize the language model with RL, using the reward model to rate what it generates. 8

RLHF is now one part of post-training, a many-stage process built from three optimization methods. 3

MethodWhat it doesWhat it is largely about
Instruction fine-tuning (IFT or SFT)Teaches formatting and forms the base of instruction followingFeatures in language
Preference fine-tuning (PreFT)Aligns to human preferences, through RLHF and related methodsStyle, and subtle preferences that are hard to quantify
RL with verifiable rewards (RLVR)Boosts performance on verifiable domains with more RL trainingThe newest of the three

RLHF lives inside preference tuning and dominates it. 11 Direct alignment methods sit beside it, and "post-training" covers everything after large-scale pretraining. 12

Why is it still the RLHF book? Because RLHF is changing more slowly, which makes it a useful snapshot of the methods and the core problems. 14 It is also where the attention started, as the technique behind ChatGPT's success. 15

What does RLHF do?

In Lecture 1: 9:58 · 11:31 · 34:17

Lambert calls this the biggest question around RLHF, and one that is still hard to answer. 16 He starts with one prompt, "The president of the united states in 2006 was", on two models from the same lineage. 17

Base model: Llama 3.1 405B Base

George W. Bush, the governor of Florida in 2006 was Jeb Bush, and John McCain was an Arizona senator in 2006 - who later lost to obama.

It completes the sentence and adds internet-style metadata. 18

After post-training: Tülu 3 405B

George W. Bush was the president of the United States in 2006. He served two terms in office, from January 20, 2001, to January 20, 2009.

The book calls this a change in style, and credits post-training as a whole, not RLHF alone. 19

Style and format are the chapter's two lenses. Style is the manner of an answer: a blunt reply and a warm reply to "I'm panicking about a presentation" differ in style. Format is the structure: one sentence versus a plan with headings and lists. 20 Instruction tuning supplies the question-answering ability, and RLHF turns those answers into reliable, warm, engaging ones. 21

Why the two behave differently comes down to the optimization. 22 23 24

Instruction fine-tuningRLHF
Unit of updatePer token: predict the next tokenPer response: tune whole completions
What the model is toldA specific response to learnWhat a better response looks like, and what to avoid
LossNext-token predictionContrastive: computed by comparing examples
GeneralizationWeaker"Far better across domains"
CostSimpleMore compute, data and time

The catch is control. The reward signal is at best a proxy, so the optimization is prone to over-optimization and needs regularization. RLHF also needs a strong starting point, so it cannot solve every problem alone. 25

Walkthrough of an RLHF recipe

In Lecture 1: 24:26 · 25:27 · 26:31

This is the chapter's jargon-free version of InstructGPT's 2022 recipe. 2 Follow it on Figure 1.

Instruction tuning teaches the shape of an answerA base model only completes text. Training it with the same next-token loss, but only on question-answering data, turns it into an instruction follower. 26 In InstructGPT's time people wrote these completions by hand. Batch size and learning rate are also smaller, so the model learns the format without overfitting to it. 27
The reward model turns preferences into a scoreRL needs a single number that indicates quality. A language model, usually the instruction-tuned one, is fine-tuned on preference pairs, and can then score any text. 28
RL makes good tokens more likelyTake prompts, generate many completions, have the reward model rank them, and use RL to update the model. This repeats, keeping the model's general capabilities. Once performance saturates, this is often the model users get. 29

The chapter deliberately has no equations. The objective behind step 3 is in what the lecture adds.

An intuition for post-training

In Lecture 1: 34:17 · 35:48 · 38:55

The elicitation theory says post-training extracts potential already in the base model, by amplifying its valuable behaviors. 30 Lambert's analogy is Formula 1: the base model is the chassis, and the best teams improve more during a season than from one chassis to the next. 31 His example is OLMoE, where a second version that only updated post-training scored higher on benchmarks. 32 The exact figures vary between his sources (see below).

How we got here

In Lecture 1: 33:46

2023: the Alpaca eraOpen groups tuned the original LLaMA on a few human datapoints plus Self-Instruct-style synthetic data, and judged results largely on vibes. 45 46
Then doubtMany companies questioned whether they needed RLHF, and "instruction tuning is enough for alignment" became a common phrase. Those that embraced RLHF early won out. 47
Late 2023: the DPO eraDPO solves the same problem with fewer moving parts, taking gradient steps directly on preference pairs. The May 2023 paper made little impact until models with a lower learning rate, such as Zephyr-Beta and Tülu 2. 48
2024: another slumpOpen recipes stalled, while the Llama 3.1 and Nemotron reports hinted that large-scale post-training is far more complex. Tülu 3 was a comprehensive open effort to lay a foundation for academic research. 49
Now: RLVR and reasoningThese build on RLHF's infrastructure and ideas but evolve far faster. 50 His slides label 2026 as agentic training and multi-turn RL. 46

Future of RLHF

In Lecture 1: 43:36

Lambert expects RLHF to be seen as the bridge to wider use of RL for fine-tuning base models. Its lasting core is a harder question: how to map human values into systems people use every day. 56 The lecture closes the same way, saying that capturing preferences stays in these models. 57

What the lecture adds beyond the book

Lecture 1 covers Chapters 1–3, so some of this returns in the next two chapters.

Why preferences at all. Helpfulness is easy to judge and hard to write as a formula, so RLHF optimizes behavior you can evaluate but cannot specify. 58 His example before language models is a backflip: an agent trained on human feedback beat one with a hand-written reward. 59

The objective behind step 3. 60

$$\max_{\pi} \; \mathbb{E}_{x \sim D,\; y \sim \pi(\cdot \mid x)} \underbrace{r_\phi(x, y)}_{\text{maximize the reward}} - \underbrace{\beta D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(\pi(\cdot \mid x)\,\|\,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(\cdot \mid x)\right)}_{\text{but don't change the model too much}}$$
  • $x$ is a prompt from the dataset $D$, and $y$ is a completion from the policy $\pi$, the model being trained.
  • $r_\phi(x, y)$ is the learned reward model's score from step 2.
  • $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is the reference model, which anchors the policy to the SFT model. 61
  • $\beta$ sets the trade-off between improving behavior and staying close to what the model already knows. 61

Change the model too much in early RLHF and it "degrades into total yapping". 62 For why RLVR often drops this term anyway, see the earlier answer.

How RLHF differs from classical RL. 63

Classical RLRLHFRLVR
RewardEnvironmentLearned (proxy)Verifiable (exact)
State transitionsYesNoNo
Reward granularityPer-stepPer-responsePer-response
Primary challengeExplore-exploit trade-offOver-optimizationTask generalization

From one pass to many rounds. The same pieces now run in a loop with more stages, data and filtering. 64 He says you could have 5 or even 20 intermediate model versions. 65 Data grew too: roughly 10K instruction and 100K preference examples for InstructGPT, and about 1M of each for Tülu 3. 10

Three ways to tune preferences all target the same objective. 66

Rejection samplingOnline RL (PPO)DPO
MechanismFilter, then SFTGenerate, score, update policyDirect gradient on preferences
Reward modelRequiredRequiredImplicit
ComplexityLowHighLow

He credits DPO with about 80% or more of final performance at far lower cost. 67 On rejection sampling he is candid: people use it and it works well, but he hasn't gotten it to work himself. 68

What over-optimization looks like: reward scores climbing while quality falls, verbosity, sycophancy and over-refusal. The KL penalty is the main defense. 69

Check yourself

Your tuned model keeps saying "As a language model trained by…". Which stage do you look at first?

The instruction tuning data. Lambert says that phrase normally means the data contains too much of it, and you can remove it. 78 It fits that stage's job of teaching specific features. 3

Why not just write a reward function for "helpful"?

Because helpfulness is easy to judge and hard to specify. 58 The price is that a learned reward model is only a proxy, which is why over-optimization is the central risk. 25

You know exactly which output distribution you want. SFT or RLHF?

SFT: when you know the target, he calls instruction tuning the easiest way, efficient and simple. 79 RLHF earns its cost when you need behavior that generalizes across domains. 23 24

A colleague says post-training is just style, so a thousand examples will do. What is right and wrong about that?

Right: a few thousand samples can change a model substantially. Wrong: that improves a narrow set of evaluations and doesn't always carry over to harder capabilities. 80 Changing the data can matter far more. 34

What would you expect from an early RLHF run with no KL term?

Nothing limits how far the model moves from what it already knew. In early RLHF, changing it too much meant repeated characters and inconsistent language, 62 and reward scores can climb while quality falls. 69

Does the elicitation theory describe a frontier RL run?

Not fully. He says it is likely right only for lighter recipes, 39 and that it breaks down as RL scales. 40 Whether that RL teaches new abilities is his stated open question. 38
