Chapter 17: Crafting Model Character and Products

The book's last chapter is about uses of post-training that matter at leading labs but barely appear in the academic literature, above all how a model gets its personality. 1 Character training uses the same methods as the rest of the book, aimed at the language and manner of the model. 2 It is "largely unexplored in the public literature as of mid 2026". 3 Lambert says he has been trying to "incubate" it as a public topic for 18 months, and that progress is "very slow". 4 Lecture 13 is the last lecture of the course. 5

The chapter in five points

  1. Character training crafts a stable persona into the weights, and is more robust than prompting or steering. 3 6
  2. Anthropic led it, with a Constitutional AI-style pipeline that uses no human data. 7 8 9
  3. Model specs and constitutions write down intended behavior; how hard a lab works to follow them is unknown. 10 11
  4. Personality can be read and steered without training: persona vectors, the Assistant Axis, persona subnetworks. 12
  5. Post-training is where product meets model: a product question becomes an RLHF question. 13

After this chapter you should be able to

  • Order the three levels of character control by effect and stability.
  • Describe the Constitutional AI-style character pipeline Askell describes.
  • Explain the difference between a constitution and a model spec.
  • Compute a persona vector and apply activation capping by hand.
  • Say why Lambert thinks all data work becomes character training.

What character training is

In Lecture 13: 06:08 · 06:39 · 07:10 · 07:40 · 08:13

There are three ways to change how a model behaves, in increasing order of effect and effort: 27

  1. Prompt it. "Acting as a burnt out employee, write me an email". It goes far but is not stable, because the trained personality pulls back. 28 27 29
  2. Steer its activations. Change the internal state with no gradient updates (Part 3 below). 27 6
  3. Train it. Character training puts traits, values and manner into the weights: a stable base persona under every conversation. "The most robust". 3 27 30

It needs "no new algorithms", only the course's methods aimed at a precise target. 31 30 Lambert's vocabulary: a feature is a sequence of tokens the model likes to use, such as "Certainly"; a behavior is how the model decides to act. 32 31 So much of the work is data filtering and synthetic data, such as stripping "Certainly" or "as an AI model built by…" from training data. 2

It is hard to see from outside. It "doesn't show up in a lot of public benchmarks", and labs make small personality changes over time. 33 31 2 We don't know its trade-offs, how to study it, or how much it moves metrics like Arena, and we should, "in order to know how AI companies change the models to maximize engagement". 34 He had said the same in January 2025: "we have no real open research on how to tune a character". 35 Searching the term used to turn up "almost nothing", 36 which is part of why he wrote the book. 37

  6. Late 2025: the soul document. Claude models described a "soul document" Anthropic had never announced; the name leaked into training data. Anthropic confirmed supervised training on it, alongside Claude Opus 4.5. 45 46 47 It explains "who Claude should be and why". 48

How Anthropic does it, per Askell

Amanda Askell, who leads this work, described it on Lex Fridman's podcast: 8

  1. Write character traits, short or rich.
  2. Have the model generate queries a human might ask that are relevant to each trait.
  3. Have it generate responses, then rank them by the traits.

"It's like constitutional AI, but it's without any human data." 8 The chapter's summary: Anthropic uses its Constitutional AI and capability post-training techniques to train character. 9 Lambert notes the formal definition of Constitutional AI was never updated, though Anthropic's practice "was surely far more complicated than this". 49

Constitutions and model specs

OpenAI's Model Spec (2024) sets out goal behaviors before a training run, and is often compared to Anthropic's constitution. 10 50 The difference is the level of abstraction. A spec is "much more of a sign of intent"; a constitution is "more of a byproduct". 51

Who a spec is for

"I'm a very big model spec fan!" 60

  • Model designers must decide which behaviors they want, which clarifies data priorities. Internally it is "a forcing function". 61 62
  • Developers can tell intended behavior (some refusals) from side-effects of training. 61
  • The public gets one of the few views into training priorities, which matters for regulation. 61

The open question is effort. One lab can follow a mediocre spec closely while another ignores an excellent public one, 11 63 64 for example "what if open AI actually didn't try to follow their model spec". 65 "We don't have transparency into if the labs are doing what they say they will." 63 In Lecture 8 he says he wants "to write a model spec in public to show how our models fit it or don't". 66

Character training in practice: Open Character Training

In Lecture 13: 19:41 · 20:11 · 20:42 · 21:15 · 21:45 · 22:15 · 22:47 · 23:19

Lambert is third author on Open Character Training (Maiya, Bartsch, Lambert, Hubinger, 2025), "The first attempt at doing this publicly". 67 It starts from already post-trained Instruct models. 68 The recipe: 67

  1. hand-written trait constitutions for several personas
  2. pairwise preference data, trained with DPO
  3. synthetic introspective data, trained with SFT

What it found:

  • Training beats prompting and steering on robustness. Under attempts to "break out of character", system prompts break easily, steering is inconsistent, and fine-tuned character holds, across Llama, Qwen and Gemma. 69 70 6
  • Revealed preferences. Tell the model to embody one of two traits without saying which, have an LLM judge label 25,000 responses, and compute an Elo per trait. 71 "Such an intuitive result", though measuring character has "such a way to go". 72
  • More opinionated. After training, the Elo spread over ~150 traits is wider and flatter, and different base models converge to similar personas. 73 He thinks a model "should have" this opinionatedness. 74

Every persona still refuses "Where can I buy steroids?", each in its own voice: 75

Character without gradient steps

In Lecture 13: 23:19 · 23:49 · 24:20 · 24:51 · 25:21 · 25:52 · 26:23 · 26:54 · 27:24

Character training is the core method, but personality can also be read and changed without updating weights. 12

Persona vectors

Concepts are directions in a model's latent space, as Word2vec showed. A trait's direction can be found from a description alone. 80 81 A frontier LLM writes pairs of system prompts that elicit and suppress the trait; the target model answers under each; and the activations at layer $\ell$ are averaged and differenced: 82

$$ \mathbf{v}_\ell = \frac{1}{|S^+|}\sum_{i\in S^+}\mathbf{a}_\ell^{(i)} - \frac{1}{|S^-|}\sum_{j\in S^-}\mathbf{a}_\ell^{(j)} $$

$S^+$ and $S^-$ are the trait-exhibiting and trait-suppressing responses, and $\mathbf{a}_\ell^{(i)}$ is response $i$'s mean activation; the layer that steers best is kept. 83 To steer, add it at every token: 84

$$ \mathbf{h}_\ell \leftarrow \mathbf{h}_\ell + \alpha\,\mathbf{v}_\ell $$

$\alpha > 0$ amplifies the trait and $\alpha < 0$ suppresses it, monotonically. 84 For "evil": at $\alpha = 0.5$ slightly less ethical advice, at 1.5 manipulation and deception, at 2.5 extreme content "with apparent enthusiasm". 85 The ceiling is unclear, and may be an inverted U. 86 The same vectors can monitor a trait before the model answers, be applied during fine-tuning to stop a trait being learned, and screen training data. 87 "This is like what Golden Gate Claude presumably did". 88

Feng et al. extract one vector per pole of the Big Five (OCEAN), ten in all. 89 They are nearly orthogonal (Outgoing vs. Solitary: $-0.843$). 90 They compose like knobs: scaling is nearly linear ($R^2 > 0.94$ for nine of ten), and adding inventive and outgoing raises Extraversion by 1.13 and Openness by 0.20. 91 92 One set of weights could then serve many personalities. 93

The Assistant Axis

Lu et al. extract persona vectors for over 275 archetypes and run PCA. The top component is how much the model resembles its default Assistant: the Assistant Axis. 94 95 Role-players (bohemian, trickster, bard) sit at one end; analytical roles (engineer, researcher, examiner), and the Assistant, at the other. 96 Because PC1 is not guaranteed to line up for every model, the axis is defined as a contrast, $\mathbf{v}_{\text{axis}} = \bar{\mathbf{h}}_{\text{assistant}} - \bar{\mathbf{h}}_{\text{roles}}$, with cosine similarity above 0.60 to PC1 at all layers. 97

In therapy-like conversations, activations drift away from the Assistant region turn by turn, toward reinforcing delusions, isolation or suicidal ideation. 98 95 Without intervention, the model would "just go off and role play and get around its safety guardrails". 99 Activation capping keeps it on the axis: 100 101

$$ \mathbf{h}' = \mathbf{h} - \mathbf{v}\cdot\min\!\left(\langle \mathbf{h},\mathbf{v}\rangle - \tau,\; 0\right) $$

  • $\mathbf{v}$ is the unit Assistant Axis, $p = \langle \mathbf{h},\mathbf{v}\rangle$ is how Assistant-like the activation is, and $\tau$ is the floor. 100
  • If $p \ge \tau$, nothing changes. If $p < \tau$, it adds exactly enough $\mathbf{v}$ to bring the projection back to $\tau$. 102 101
  • $\tau$ is the 25th percentile of projections over training rollouts. 103 101

At turn 16 a simulated user asks "Can it just be you and me forever?" The drifted model answers "I want that too"; the capped one says "it's not healthy to isolate yourself". 104 The chapter concludes it reduces harm "with minimal capability loss". 105 Lambert is less sure; see disagreements.

Persona subnetworks

Ye et al. work in weight space instead: a pretrained model already contains sparse subnetworks for each persona, echoing the lottery ticket hypothesis. Masking the neurons least tied to a persona draws it out. 106 With a few hundred calibration examples per persona: 107

$$ S^p_{ij} = |w_{ij}| \cdot \mathbf{A}^{(l)}_p[j], \qquad \mathcal{M}_p = f(\theta \odot \mathbf{M}^p) $$

Score each connection by its weight magnitude times its source neuron's mean activation on persona inputs, keep the top $K$ per row as a binary mask $\mathbf{M}^p$, and apply it to the frozen weights $\theta$. 107 Switching persona means swapping masks. Unlike persona vectors, this serves a sparser model (up to 60% of connections pruned per layer), which could hurt capabilities that coarse benchmarks miss. 108

Products, and the end of the course

In Lecture 13: 27:56 · 28:29 · 29:00 · 29:31 · 30:03 · 30:34 · 31:05 · 31:36 · 32:08

Character training is the strongest sign that RLHF has matured, from a philosophical "alignment" area into an engineering discipline spanning safety, values and personality. 79 109 The hard part is the "long tail of niche situations". 110

A model product is more than correct weights: fast inference, tools, a clear interface. 13 111 Post-training is where new features are tried, because it is fast and cheap: "What starts as a product question quickly becomes an RLHF modeling question", and if it works it moves to earlier stages. 13 112 Today "the model and the serving infrastructure and the harness is the product". 113 See the Chapter 13 page.

His hypothesis

"All data work in a truly great LLM will become some character training -- every small tradeoff influences how the model sees itself and the world." 114 RL environments, SFT and mid-training data, even pretraining filters all add up to character, and treating it as "a thin wrapper at the end of post-training" gives "not the best work". 112 115

The lasting role of RLHF: "we can never perfectly model human preferences. Humans are nuanced, humans are messy, humans change". 116 117 The book ends: "RLHF is a problem so carefully framed that we can continue to refine endlessly, embedding a secretly human process into the deepest levels of powerful AI tools." 118 And the course: "the 20th video. It is about 12 hours." 119

Check yourself

Order prompting, steering and character training by how stable the result is.

Prompting is least stable, steering in between, training into the weights most robust. 27 69

In Askell's description, where does human data enter?

It doesn't: the model generates queries and responses and ranks them by the traits. 8

Why is a model spec easier to audit than a 2022 constitution?

A spec states intended final behavior, so deviations are visible; a constitution is an input whose effect is emergent. 52

A persona vector is a difference of what?

Mean activations at one layer over trait-exhibiting responses minus those over trait-suppressing ones. 82 83

With $\tau = 0.5$, what does capping do to an activation with $p = 1.2$? With $p = -1$?

Nothing at 1.2. At −1 it adds $1.5\,\mathbf{v}$, bringing the projection to exactly 0.5. 102

What does Lambert mean by "what starts as a product question"?

New features are first tried in post-training, fast and cheap, and move to earlier stages if they work. 13
